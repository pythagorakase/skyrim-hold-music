# Bard musical traditions exploration

[Open the Hold Music listening dashboard](../dashboard/index.html) to compare finalists and existing profiles, edit audition prompts, and record listening notes. The complete candidate bank is also searchable there.

**Status:** Design candidates for the [regional music spec](bard-regional-music-spec.md). All named Tamrielic traditions below are invented proposals. Strong predominant moods are intentional; distinct sound and coherence take priority over adding an equal emotional range to every culture.

Three independent reviews considered **18 Orsimer candidates**, **22 Bosmer candidates**, and eight additional alternatives/challenges to the whole palette. The eight overlap partly with the two larger pools, so this is not a claim of 48 unique traditions. No generated audio was auditioned in this pass.

## Selected finalists

| Culture | First choice | Other finalists |
|---|---|---|
| Orsimer | Resonant Name-Songs for the strongest vocal identity | Seven-Step Oath-Songs for instrument portability; Close-Circle Songs for a later visible ensemble |
| Bosmer | Leaping tales for a broad usable identity | Hunting-call airs for focused flute/quiet music; Spinner's tales for narrative performance |

The spec holds production cores unset pending listening. There is no equal random mixture. The **Bosmer Leaping tales audition was revised from the initial seven pulses to five pulses grouped 3+2** after comparison with Orsimer Seven-Step. The historical idea tables below retain the initial proposal to make the alternatives legible; use the spec's current fragments for auditions.

## Orsimer candidate bank

| Candidate | Audible design and predominant mood | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Resonant Name-Songs | Sustained fundamental; upper harmonics move in small deliberate patterns; long vowels; sparse plucks. Concentrated, grave, enduring. | Finalist 1. Strongest timbral signature. |
| 2. Seven-Step Oath-Songs | Seven equal pulses grouped 2+2+3; syllabic calls; hard rests; low dry lute strokes. Resolute and communal. | Finalist 2. Best across all instrument families. |
| 3. Close-Circle Songs | A lead syllabic line against two or three compact chordal response parts; rough bass timbre; synchronized releases. Intimate, solemn solidarity. | Finalist 3. Strong ensemble reward, honest solo limitation. |
| 4. Road-Debt Epics | Long prose-like verse on a two-note reciting formula; freely timed pauses; returning plucked refrain. Stubborn, weathered narrative. | Reserve. Too close to Eastmarch declamation without a new timbral feature. |
| 5. Contest of Names | Two alternating soloists progressively compress verses and shorten rests; repeating lute figure. Competitive and dryly witty. | Reserve social form. Requires two visible singers and overlaps improvised bard contests elsewhere. |
| 6. Forge Counts | A recurring pattern of three attacks and one silent slot; changing accents over a stable beat; clipped words. Focused, laboring. | Reserve occasion. Do not generate actual anvils or hammer sounds without visible sources. Too close to generic work music as a culture core. |
| 7. Descending Stone Laments | Falling fourths; a held final low note; long unmetered phrases with minimal accompaniment. Stoic grief. | Reject as core: Breton folk, Dunmer and Falkreath already occupy this space. |
| 8. Ridge Calls | Wide vocal leaps with audible chest/head-register changes; answering flute figures and spacious pauses. Vigilant, far-carrying. | Strong alternate, particularly if Bosmer avoids register-break calls. Could become a northern vocal signature instead. |
| 9. Knot Dances | Uneven five-pulse phrases, short repeated motifs, sharply articulated lute melody, abrupt cadences. Tense, physically alert. | Reserve. Seven-Step offers a clearer single rhythmic fingerprint; do not proliferate adjacent odd meters immediately. |
| 10. Embersong | Whispered or murmured narrow melody over very quiet lute pulses; long suspensions. Protective, private. | Reserve intimate mode. Too near Hjaalmarch hush and winterhold sparsity as a core. |
| 11. Returning Ancestors | A refrain accumulates one new note each verse, then collapses to its original cell; alternating high/low lute registers. Ritual remembrance. | Interesting formal variant, but cumulative form is unlikely to be recognized quickly in generated clips. |
| 12. Burden-and-Release | Two increasingly dense phrases followed by a bar of silence; heavy/quiet dynamic contrast; three-note melody. Defiant, unyielding. | Reserve. Useful arrangement device inside Seven-Step rather than a separate tradition. |
| 13. Resting Herd Songs | Long, unhurried pentatonic melody; sustained vowels; soft rocking accompaniment. Spacious contentment. | Reject as core: overlaps Khajiit drone and northern open-ended vocal styles. |
| 14. Soot-Black Virtuosity | Fast repeated-note plucking, tight cross-string figures, abrupt switches of register, dry close sound. Fierce technical concentration. | Strong instrumental alternate. Risks merging with Redguard plucked virtuosity unless ornament and meter stay rigorously different. |
| 15. Witness Recitation | Spoken rhythmic testimony gradually turns to one sustained sung pitch; single-string answers. Authoritative, austere. | Reserve special occasion. Distinct form, but music generation and SkyrimNet spoken delivery may not align. |
| 16. Long-Breath Flute | Low breathy tone alternates with clear higher harmonics; slow pitch changes over one repeated finger pattern. Contemplative and resonant. | Companion to Resonant Name-Songs, not independent core; avoid asking a normal single flute to supply an extra bass part. |
| 17. Shieldless Boasts | Brisk syllabic verses, broad upward fourths, a snapped final syllable, plain diatonic melody. Proud, convivial confidence. | Reserve lighter mode. Too near Nord tavern song to define the palette. |
| 18. Broken-Line Canon | Three staggered entries repeat short lines at different phrase lengths; clashes resolve together every several cycles. Insistent, interdependent. | Reject as core: collides with Argonian interlocking and Hjaalmarch canon; ensemble requirement also high. |

## Bosmer candidate bank

These are deliberately different hypotheses, including ideas that should lose. Similar imagery alone does not make two musical designs distinct.

| # | Proposed tradition | Audible premise | Mood / place in the palette | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leaping tales | Springing seven-unit pulse grouped 2+2+3; short melody leaps a fourth or sixth, then stops; dry plucks; quick syllabic delivery. | Mischievous, poised, alert. A compelling ordinary Bosmer song grammar. | **Initial version; current finalist revised to 3+2; production default pending.** |
| 2 | Hunting-call airs | Two- or three-note calls separated by complete silence; high and middle registers answer in sequence; clear tone and a clipped ending. | Attentive, spare, faintly predatory. | **Finalist.** |
| 3 | Spinner's tales | Speech follows a recurring three-note figure; each return adds or removes a beat; abrupt changes of register distinguish narrative turns. | Absorbing, intimate, uncanny. | **Finalist.** |
| 4 | Feast steps | Lively 3+3+2 pulse, open plucked intervals, a small refrain, sharply punctuated endings. | Physical, sociable, celebratory. | Reserve: reliable but less singular than #1; can collapse into familiar tavern dance. |
| 5 | Canopy exchanges | Two voices pass individual notes or syllables back and forth, assembling one agile line. | Playful mutual attention. | Ensemble reserve: distinguish with gaps and exchanged phrases, but too close to Argonian interlocking if made a core. |
| 6 | Register games | One singer switches chest and head register on wide leaps; sparse plucks mark only the low notes. | Daring, competitive, gleeful. | Technique reserve: strong sound, but a reference label such as yodel may dominate the worldbuilding or produce Alpine pastiche. |
| 7 | Quiet bow songs | Repeated low pluck answered by a high harmonic; spare three-note melody with long decays. | Patient, inward. | Reserve: good solo string piece, weaker group identity; risks merging with northern austerity. |
| 8 | Thorn dances | Fast five-unit pulse; tight ornaments, abruptly cut notes, sudden whole-beat rests. | Prickly excitement. | Reserve alternate to #1; do not launch both before testing their audible separation. |
| 9 | Root cradle songs | Soft rocking duple motion; descending thirds, limited range, warm unadorned voice. | Tender, secure. | Later nuance: broadly plausible but does not solve the distinctness problem first. |
| 10 | Rain patterns | Repeated five-note cells, overlapping high and low figures, continuous regular pulse. | Immersive, cyclical. | Reject as Bosmer default: already belongs to the Argonian design territory. |
| 11 | The long listening | Unmetered breathy flute, narrow register, long silence and almost no cadence. | Meditative, remote. | Reject as default: too close to the Pale/Winterhold and Dunmer flute treatments. #2 uses precise calls and register contrast instead. |
| 12 | Boasting contests | A singer delivers a rhythmically crowded line followed by an intentionally tiny instrumental answer; each verse lengthens. | Comic, competitive. | Strong later occasion; #1 can contain it without a separate core. |
| 13 | Knot refrains | A short melody gains a note or rhythm on each return, finally unravelling back to the original. | Curious, anticipatory. | Reserve composition form: distinctive on paper; a generative model may not obey the cumulative rule audibly. |
| 14 | Hunting procession | Slow dotted five-beat tread; narrow, firmly projected chant; flute or string answers land on the same pitch. | Ceremonial resolve. | Reject as default: overlaps Orc collective pulse and Nord declamation. |
| 15 | Shadow mourning | Widely spaced plucks interrupt a melody at uncomfortable places; words end before expected resolutions. | Bereft, unsettled. | Later minority register: viable and distinctive by phrasing, but the existing palette already has many laments. |
| 16 | Court mimicry | An elegant symmetrical air repeatedly sprouts an extra beat, a register jump or an unexpected stop. | Witty, irreverent. | Traveling/individual repertoire: relies on knowing the court style being subverted. |
| 17 | Shared breath | Two flutes dovetail long tones, each enters as the other stops, producing one uninterrupted line. | Intimate, suspended. | Ensemble reserve: animation and breath staging difficult; the result erases our useful crisp/gapped fingerprint. |
| 18 | Skin and syllables | One performer alternates consonant-rich short sung phrases and muted finger taps in irregular groups. | Earthy, conversational. | Drum/voice branch of #3, not a fourth core. |
| 19 | Night harmonics | Sparse high flute overtones or plucked harmonics arranged in disjunct little constellations. | Luminous, watchful. | Instrumental reserve: can support #2; risk of generic magical ambience if heavily reverberant. |
| 20 | Returning story | Melody and rhythm reverse order halfway through, making a narrative circle with an exact final return. | Intricate, satisfying. | Reject initial implementation: fragile prompt compliance; formal complexity may sound like Altmer cleverness. |
| 21 | Bough rustle | Rapid tremolo fades into soft repeated notes and returns in waves. | Hushed motion. | Reject as identity: generic texture and possible Khajiit/Redguard string overlap; also tempts the model to add environmental sound. |
| 22 | Animal portraits | A human voice uses distinct contours, articulation and registers to characterize animals in a tale. | Theatrical, amused, sometimes menacing. | Later narrator skill: musical characterization, not literal animal recordings or a claim about magical shapechanging. |

## Independent reviewer alternatives

This is the independent reviewer’s own shortlist before synthesis, not a second set of selected production finalists. Scores are comparative design judgments, not test results. D = distinctiveness against the present spec, A = compatibility with the existing visible lute/flute/drum grammar, each out of 5. Voice-only arrangements remain conditional on verifying an appropriate animation; voice with a lute is currently the easier visual route. Music-model accuracy is a separate unknown.

| Candidate | Musical proposition | Predominant effect and imagined use | D / A | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Orsimer: Stone resonance** | One low sustained voice carrying a clear upper overtone melody; very slow harmonic change; a few dry plucked punctuation notes, not a constant percussion groove. | Concentrated, solemn, patient. Formal recollection, oaths, quiet evening performance. The technique conveys discipline and fine control. | 5 / 4 | **Finalist.** The strongest one-performer expansion and the best home for overtone singing. Requires successful audio audition; do not fake it with a choir. |
| **Orsimer: Uneven measure** | A repeating 2+2+3 pulse; clipped low notes answered by ascending open intervals; abrupt clean phrase endings; restrained volume. | Poised, tenacious, exact. Music for celebrations or shared work where precision matters more than aggression. | 5 / 5 | **Finalist.** Fully viable instrumentally; audible even with the same lute and no singing. |
| **Orsimer: Quiet filigree** | Fine, close-miked rapid plucked ornaments above widely separated bass notes; short figures varied minutely each return; no harmonic haze. | Private pride, concentration, contained intensity. A craftsperson's intimate virtuoso tradition, not actual hammer effects. | 3 / 5 | Reserve. Attractive counterexample to the heavy-drum trope, but risks Altmer overlap. Make variation of one motif, not independent contrapuntal lines. |
| **Orsimer: The long answer** | A nearly spoken low opening line followed by an unexpectedly wide rising melody; measured pauses; a recurring warm open interval instead of a minor lament cadence. | Resolute tenderness, endurance, recollection. Domestic or parting songs. | 3 / 5 | Reserve. Gives emotional breadth later but competes with Eastmarch declamation and Dunmer lament now. |
| **Bosmer: Turning tales** | Fast text-like syllables; sudden register switches as one performer changes character; short asymmetric phrases; instrumental punchlines after the voice stops. | Wry, mercurial, alert. Story contests, comic accounts, clever recollection. | 5 / 5 | **Finalist.** Give it rhythmic and melodic identity even without literal lyrics. One voice changes register; no invisible cast. |
| **Bosmer: Leaping dance** | Crisp detached notes, wide interval jumps, a recurring 3+2+2 pattern, short silences before the final accent, quick question/answer between vocal and instrumental phrases. | Springy, incisive, playful but not pastoral sweetness. Social dance and tavern display. | 5 / 5 | **Finalist.** Works on lute, flute or drum. Distinguish from Orc uneven measure through register, articulation, contour and speed. |
| **Bosmer: High calls** | An exposed high melodic call drops suddenly into a low answer by the same singer, with long quiet gaps; little harmonic accompaniment. | Uncanny, watchful, spatial. An imagined signaling tradition developed into music, with no claim about canonical hunting practices. | 4 / 3 | Reserve. Strong color but may merge with northern sparse vocables; large vocal jumps are an audio-generation test. |
| **Bosmer: Close shadow** | Nearly whispered rhythmic speech alternating with sharply plucked high notes; two neighboring pitches recurring against an unexpected low anchor; phrases cut off just before expected closure. | Cunning, intimate, slightly unsettling. Small-room storytelling and dark humor. | 4 / 4 | Reserve. Good alternate mood, but avoid Hjaalmarch's sustained neighbor-tone haze. This must stay dry and punctual. |

The reviewer’s proposed pair of principal profiles was **Stone resonance plus Uneven measure** for Orsimer and **Turning tales plus Leaping dance** for Bosmer. Each culture gets a voice-specialist branch and an immediately useful instrumental branch, without trying to express all possible moods at once. Candidate names are working labels for original fictional forms, not translations of real cultural names.
