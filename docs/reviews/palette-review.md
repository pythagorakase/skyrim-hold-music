# Independent musical palette review

> Pre-synthesis review, retained for research history. The [current spec](../bard-regional-music-spec.md) supersedes candidate selections and prompt fragments below.

Reviewed 2026-10-08 against [regional music spec](../bard-regional-music-spec.md), before the current brainstorming edits. This report proposes music for a fictional setting; it does not claim that these styles are established Elder Scrolls canon. It follows the user's priority: distinct, coherent predominant palettes first; minority moods and individual exceptions later. No shared spec or game files were edited by this reviewer.

## Judgment

Keep most of the current mappings. Their weakness is not their predominant moods. The weakness is that several different reference labels currently produce almost the same audible instructions. Solve those collisions with phrase length, articulation, meter, register, ornament and cadence. A second happy Dunmer preset is lower priority than making the existing Dunmer lament recognizably different from Breton and Falkreath laments.

The largest additions should occupy two comparatively empty spaces: **Orsimer: resonant, weighty, architecturally precise**; **Bosmer: agile, abrupt, theatrical, asymmetrical**. These can be rich musical cultures with strong default moods before we add dozens of exceptions.

## Audible collisions and repairs

| Pair or cluster | What would currently sound alike | Smallest useful repair |
|---|---|---|
| Redguard / Khajiit | Drone, free opening, sliding melodic line, melisma, cyclic pulse and increasing speed. Reference names alone may not separate them. | Redguard: articulated attacks, clear repeating meter once established, sharply outlined ornaments and returning cadences. Khajiit: sustained connected notes, delayed pulse, elastic development and broad continuous slides. Keep both ornamented. |
| Dunmer / Breton folk / Falkreath | Slow minor lament, sparse accompaniment, descending phrases, free or weak pulse. | Dunmer keeps the sorrowful long-breathed melisma and augmented-second color. Breton folk favors clear syllables, long text-led stanzas, repeated melodic line, little ornament. Falkreath favors short descending cells in a perceptible five-beat cycle and a narrow range. All may stay sad. |
| Imperial / Breton court / Haafingar | Polished lute song, pleasant chords, grace notes, formal cadence. | Imperial owns rhetorical solo melody, expressive tempo, changing harmony and strong arrival points. Breton court owns restrained syllabic intimacy and little graces. Haafingar owns a recognizable triple dance pulse and spacious instrumental answers. |
| Altmer / other court music | 'Learned', 'precise' and 'balanced' may yield generic tasteful lute music. | Preserve the independent moving lines and imitation as the defining feature. On one flute, use sequential imitation of the performer's previous phrase, not simultaneous polyphony. |
| Winterhold / Pale | Their profiles are literally identical. | Retain Winterhold's sparse circular vocable melody. Give the Pale long, exposed, separated calls and a slow rocking compound pulse; make the northern coast audible without requiring a second singer. Do not give it Orsimer overtone bass by default. |
| Argonian / Hjaalmarch | Small repeating pitch set, hypnotic cells, unresolved endings. | Argonian owns crisp alternating registers and a clearly recurring rhythmic cycle. Hjaalmarch owns slow sustained adjacent tones, friction, hushed attacks and unstable endings. Distinguish articulation and density, not happiness. |
| Whiterun / Rift | Familiar modal tavern refrain and dance lilt. | Keep Whiterun's broad triple sway and clear storytelling. Keep the Rift quick, tightly packed, bouncing compound meter and almost immediate refrain return. No need to replace either. |
| Reach profile internally | Free-rhythm heavily ornamented singing and quick nonsense-syllable dance rhythm are two different starts. | Choose a song type per performance: ornamented air OR quick mouth-music dance. An opening air leading into a dance is possible, but should be an intentional arrangement, not contradictory simultaneous instructions. |

Some overlap is useful. Haafingar should plausibly share prestige vocabulary with Imperial and Breton court repertories. The goal is a listener recognizing a dominant palette, not every tradition avoiding every interval used by its neighbors.

## Preserve these decisions

- **Dunmer as predominantly mournful.** It is coherent and evocative. Keep rare celebratory variants for later, without weakening the core. Gray Quarter and Raven Rock can share this core.
- **Altmer counterpoint.** It gives the plucked-string animation something structurally different to do.
- **Khajiit sustained ornament and moon-linked selection.** Keep the musician's core identity while the moon chooses a condition within it.
- **Argonian cyclical interlocking.** Strong contrast with text-led Nord ballads. A solo instrument can alternate registers; true simultaneous independent performers wait for a visible roster.
- **Eastmarch's austere declamation.** Its lack of decorative excess is valuable palette space.
- **Whiterun as the familiar center.** The baseline makes the unusual places legible.
- **Rift exuberance.** A strong happy/default region is welcome alongside several dark ones.

Voice-only arrangements remain conditional on verifying an appropriate animation; voice with a lute is currently the easier visual route. Music-model accuracy is a separate unknown.

| **Bosmer: Close shadow** | Nearly whispered rhythmic speech alternating with sharply plucked high notes; two neighboring pitches recurring against an unexpected low anchor; phrases cut off just before expected closure. | Cunning, intimate, slightly unsettling. My pair of principal profiles would be **Stone resonance plus Uneven measure** for Orsimer and **Turning tales plus Leaping dance** for Bosmer. Each culture gets a voice-specialist branch and an immediately useful instrumental branch, without trying to express all possible moods at once. Candidate names are working labels for original fictional forms, not translations of real cultural names.

## Six high-value changes

1. **Give Orc and Bosmer actual idioms now.** Bosmer should not fall back to no culture layer merely because the visible prop is wooden. Its musical grammar can work on a lute regardless of later material choices. Treat Green Pact instrument preferences as a character/material policy to verify, not the entire culture's music.
2. **Separate Redguard from Khajiit with articulation and time.** This repairs the most serious cross-culture collision without replacing either inspiration.
3. **Differentiate the lament family without making it cheerful.** Preserve Dunmer's strong mood; change the phrase mechanics of Breton folk and Falkreath.
4. **Split Winterhold/Pale and Argonian/Hjaalmarch.** The former is duplication; the latter needs crisp cycles versus slow dissonant suspension.
5. **Replace the College override with an available learned repertoire.** Training can make court forms selectable without changing every trained musician into Haafingar. An explicit tradition chosen for a performance should resolve before the location tint.
6. **Make geography support the requested world.** Replace hold-only scope with regions: the nine holds, Cyrodiil, and distinct Solstheim communities. Give Cyrodiil a vernacular Imperial profile; keep Gray Quarter/Raven Rock connected; assign Skaal independently. Location must not overwrite every resident's training.

## Regional additions and throat singing placement

**Cyrodiil:** Start with a vernacular companion to the existing Imperial monody: firm regular pulse, memorable short refrain, clear major/minor harmonic motion, strongly articulated cadences, quick ornamental pickups. A street or roadside performance should be extroverted and immediately legible; court monody keeps flexible speech-shaped timing. This preserves family resemblance while creating a meaningful vernacular. Further Colovian/Nibenese division can wait; do not merely call Haafingar music 'Cyrodiilic'.

**Raven Rock / Gray Quarter:** Use the same Dunmeri root. Distinction can begin with arrangement density or repertory selection rather than a new ethnic palette. Raven Rock need not become Nordic simply because the worldspace is Solstheim.

**Skaal:** A separate northern register can use unadorned long calls, sparse pulse and exposed silence. Do not assign throat singing broadly to Skaal, Pale, Winterhold, Orc and Argonian simultaneously in v1; the technique loses its identifying power. One later named Skaal singer could share it through an authored tradition or personal training.

**Overtone singing:** Put sustained bass plus clearly shaped upper harmonics in the Orsimer specialist profile first. A single performer can produce the simultaneous effect; this does not authorize adding a second voice in the generated arrangement. UNESCO describes this one-performer capability in its account of [Mongolian khoomei](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mongolian-art-of-singing-khoomei-00210?RL=00210). We borrow an acoustic technique for an invented form; we should not claim a real-world tradition is canonical Orc culture.

**Avoid confusing other resonant forms with solo overtones.** Sardinian canto a tenore is a four-part group form, according to [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/canto-a-tenore-sardinian-pastoral-songs-00165). It could be an exciting later Orc ensemble reference but would be a bad solo prompt reference. Keep its name out until there are enough visible singers. Similarly, rhythmic paired vocal games require the matching visible pair; a technique name is not a license for invisible performers.

## Underused dimensions and audition criteria

The spec presently spends many words on mode, drone and mood. It underuses **attack versus sustain, phrase length, melodic contour, register span, stable versus shifting accents, cadence strength, silence placement, and how voice relates to instrument**. These give more distinction per prompt character than adding another reference tradition.

Use one stable arrangement for comparison: solo plucked instrument plus one singer, comparable clip duration and recording perspective. Then blind-compare the risky pairs. Listen for distinctive features, not whether the prompt labels are different. In particular:

- Can a listener separate Redguard's articulated cyclic phrases from Khajiit connected development?
- Can a listener distinguish three sad songs by their phrase shapes?
- Can Bosmer remain identifiable when the singer is absent?
- Can Orsimer remain identifiable without forceful drums or a low male voice stereotype?
- Does Stone resonance produce one continuous singer with upper harmonics, or does the model smuggle in backing voices?
- Does the exact roster match every audible sound source? A flute player cannot simultaneously sing a sustained independent line while blowing; alternate the phrases or provide a second visible singer.

The proposed candidates remain untested. A flat failure to generate true solo overtones should fall back to the instrumental Orsimer branch, not silently enlarge the ensemble.
